Bring the part

Half the work here arrives as a part, not a vehicle: the pump, the injectors or the turbo comes in, and it leaves rebuilt and tested.

  • Injection pumps. Diesel fuel injection pumps rebuilt and tested here, whether it is a mechanic's customer unit, a farm's tractor pump or a generator set.
  • Injectors. Rebuilt and tested the same way, in a full set or one at a time.
  • Turbochargers. Brought in with the rest of the diesel work; call with the number off the tag if you are matching one up.
  • Parts counter. A walk-in counter for mechanics, farms and generator owners picking up a pump, a set of injectors or a turbo.

Call first with the part number if you have it.

Bring the truck

The other half drives in. Leave the truck and let the shop find the fault before anything gets replaced.

  • Engine diagnostics. Read the fault first, so a part only gets replaced when it is the part at fault.
  • Injector replacement. Pulling the old injectors and fitting the new ones with the engine still in the truck.
  • Turbo repair. For a turbo that is smoking, leaking or not making boost.
  • Basic maintenance. Oil changes and the routine service a diesel needs between bigger jobs.
  • Fleet service. Several trucks at once, scheduled around the days they are off the road.
